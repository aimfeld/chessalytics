<task type="checkpoint:human-verify" gate="blocking">
  <name>Task 2: Operator UAT of the redesigned reveal on desktop and mobile</name>
  <what-built>
The Train post-solve reveal, rebuilt as a self-contained study surface: board arrows for the
good moves, the played move and the move played in the game; verdict rows stating the
answers actually given; three steppable engine line boxes each with a white-POV eval, all
from the client grading engine; an opponent-and-date footer replacing the game card; Analyze
and Next on one line with Analyze deep-linking to the puzzle position; and a running session
score on the progress bar.
  </what-built>
  <how-to-verify>
Start the dev database and both dev servers, sign in as a real account with imported and
analyzed games, and open `/train`. Start a session and work through at least four puzzles,
deliberately covering a sharp puzzle, a soft or herring puzzle, one puzzle where you play
the engine's own best move, and one where you play a clear blunder.

Check, on a desktop-width window:

1. D-02 arrows. After each verdict lands, the board shows green arrows for the good moves —
   exactly one on a sharp puzzle, at most three on a soft or herring one — plus a distinct
   arrow for the move you played and a thinner white arrow for the move played in the game.
   Confirm all three kinds are distinguishable, including when two of them land on the same
   from-to squares. Confirm no arrow of any kind appears before you have moved.
2. D-03 verdict rows. The rows name the guess you committed in words and the move you played
   in algebraic notation, each with its mark — not bare marks.
3. D-03 line boxes. Three boxes appear: your move, the best move, and the move played in the
   game, each with its own eval. Step each one with the arrows and by clicking a move token,
   and confirm the shared board follows and no second board appears. Confirm the numbers are
   mutually consistent: a move the verdict called incorrect must not show a better eval than
   the best move.
4. D-03 coincidence. On the puzzle where you played the engine's best move, confirm ONE box
   appears carrying both role labels rather than two identical boxes.
5. D-03 footer and actions. The full game card is gone; a small footer names the opponent and
   the game date; Analyze and Next sit on one line. Press Analyze and confirm the analysis
   board opens on the SAME position the puzzle showed you — not one half-move later.
6. D-01 latency. The your-move and best-move boxes render immediately; the played-in-game box
   shows a brief spinner and then fills in. Note roughly how long it takes.
7. D-04 score. The running score sits at the top-right of the progress bar. Reload the page
   mid-session, resume, and confirm the score continues from where it was rather than
   restarting at zero.
8. Failure honesty. With the network throttled or offline, confirm no box hangs forever and
   each failure states itself.

Then narrow the window to a phone width (or use device emulation) and repeat items 1, 2, 3,
5 and 7. Everything must be present and readable; no element may be desktop-only.
  </how-to-verify>
  <resume-signal>Type "approved" to discharge the checkpoint, or describe each issue found (with the puzzle type and viewport) so it can be fixed before the phase closes.</resume-signal>
</task>
