<objective>
Turn the proven push chain into a scheduled Train reminder: a fifth periodic
lifespan task that, at least every 15 minutes, finds the users who have
reminders on and a live device, decides in Python whether it is their scheduled
day and whether their chosen local hour has arrived, settles their streak so the
day number it reports is honest, claims the day atomically before sending, and
fans one message out to every one of their devices.

Purpose: this is the requirement mass of the phase (REMIND-02 through
REMIND-07). Everything it needs — the send path, the fan-out, the notification
copy, the settings columns — already exists and is tested.

Output: `app/repositories/train_reminder_repository.py`, the candidate loop and
periodic wrapper in `app/services/train_reminder_service.py`, a `local_hour`
helper beside `local_today`, the `app/main.py` lifespan wiring, a test suite
covering every REMIND requirement, and the CHANGELOG entry for the phase.
</objective>

<open_decisions_resolved>
Two questions the source artifacts leave open, decided here rather than left to
the executor:

1. **`weekday_mask == 0` ("train anytime").** `is_scheduled_day` returns False
   for every day under a zero mask. A reminder therefore never fires for such a
   user. That is the correct reading of REMIND-03 ("fires only on a day the
   user's `weekday_mask` schedules") and it is what reusing the existing
   predicate gives for free. Do not special-case it. Pin it with a named test so
   the behaviour is deliberate rather than incidental.

2. **Where `streak_count` for the copy comes from.** `settle_streak_snapshot`
   returns a `TickView` whose `.settled` is the current `TickSnapshot` whether
   or not it changed. Use `view.settled.streak_count` — never the pre-settlement
   `TrainSettingsRow.streak_count`, and never a second SELECT. This is D-12's
   whole point: the job writes streak state and then reads its own write.
</open_decisions_resolved>
