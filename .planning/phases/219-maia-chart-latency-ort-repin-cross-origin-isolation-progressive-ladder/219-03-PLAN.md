<objective>
Paint the Human Move Probability chart from an 11-rung coarse pass and refine it in place
when the remaining rungs land, instead of holding the chart blank until all 21 have
arrived — while every consumer that needs a stable full ladder keeps waiting for one.

Purpose: after 219-01 and 219-02 the full ladder is fast, but the chart still shows
nothing until the last rung lands. maiachess.com paints 9 rungs; we paint 21 or nothing.
Splitting phase 3 into a coarse pass and a fill pass cuts perceived latency roughly in
half again without touching the ladder definition or halving chart resolution.

Output: a `perElo` that may be partial plus an explicit `isLadderComplete` flag, a
coarse/fill split in the pipeline planner, all eight `perElo` consumers explicitly
classified and the four wait-for-complete ones guarded, an invariant test that no
consumer infers completeness from length or FEN equality, and the final MAIAPERF-07
numbers.

Decisions implemented by this plan:
- D-11: the ladder definition and rung tolerance are unchanged; pipeline phase 3 splits into a coarse pass and a fill pass, both keeping the stale-FEN discard and the existing queue order (Task 1).
- D-12: `perElo` becomes ascending-and-possibly-partial with an explicit `isLadderComplete`; every consumer is enumerated and assigned to paint-live or wait-for-complete (Tasks 1 and 2).
- D-13: the per-FEN result cache and the policy cache merge rungs across both passes, and a cached complete FEN still paints in one go (Task 1).
- D-14: this plan is the third of the three sequential squash-merges and runs the full pre-merge gate plus `npm run build` (Task 4).
- D-15: measured, not asserted — all three targets are read on the reference box and reported with MET or MISSED verdicts (Task 3).
- D-16: no backend, migration or dev-database work; `bin/reset_db.sh` is never run (whole plan).
</objective>

<assumption_delta_decision>
**Noun:** ladder completeness.

**Identity shift:** until this plan, "the ladder is complete" was not a thing the system
represented — it was *inferred*, in four different places, from a proxy: `perElo.length > 0`
(non-empty implied all 21, because `buildLadder` returned `[]` otherwise), or `resultFen`
matching the requested FEN (which implied the ladder alongside it). Once the ladder can be
partial, both proxies become false. `perElo` goes from "all-or-nothing ladder" to
"partial ladder + explicit `isLadderComplete`".

**Decision: promote.** The partial ladder becomes the primary representation of `perElo`,
and completeness becomes an explicit first-class field (`isLadderComplete`) on `MaiaResult`
and on `UseMaiaEngineState`. Every consumer that needs completeness reads the flag; no
consumer may re-derive it.

**Rationale:** the proxy and the property were only ever accidentally equal, and the
accident ends here — making completeness explicit is the only change that cannot silently
misclassify a gem.

**Companion invariant test (Task 2):** feed the hook an 11-rung coarse result and assert
that the position verdict, the gem sweep and the `maiaCurveByFen` cache do NOT act on it.
</assumption_delta_decision>
