## What changed

| ply | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | ≥4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| before | 14 | 14 | 14 | 10 | 10 |
| **after** | 14 | 14 | **10** | 10 | 10 |

`GRADING_DEPTH_FLOOR` is unchanged at 10, and `GRADING_ROOT_DEPTH` still resolves to 14.
Only ply 2 moves. The table is expressed as `[14, 14]` rather than `[14, 14, 10]` because a

Both rung values remain measured Stage A rows, so D-01 ("a lookup table, never a formula")
is satisfied without interpolation.

## Why §7 is overridden

§7 tests the same three conjuncts as §4. Phase 195 overrode **§4** at Plan 05's operator
checkpoint on three grounds recorded in `findings-stage-a.md` §9.1. All three apply to §7
verbatim; §7 escaped review only because §4 was the clause under examination at the time.

1. **§7 gates on a proxy the harness disclaims.** `engine-grading-depth-ab.mjs`'s module
   header: *"This script cannot tell you a bot got weaker."* `compareToReference`
   (`:521-543`) compares FlawChess's own `practicalScore` against flat-depth-14 FlawChess.
   It measures perturbation from a prior configuration. There is no external reference in
   it, so it cannot express a quality verdict in either direction.
2. **§7's threshold sits below the measurement's own reproducibility.** §7 tests against
   0.007. The reference depth disagrees with **itself** by 0.013501 (§5), and D-07's
   warm-hash probe puts harness self-reproducibility at 0.013984 over 120 probes (115
   divergent). Every candidate §7 rejected — `L-aggressive` 0.015050, `L-graded` 0.014353,
   `L-conservative` 0.010136 — lies at or below that floor. They were rejected on a signal
   the instrument cannot resolve.
3. **§7 compares at fixed node count.** `nodes_evaluated` is 1050 at every measured depth;
   the budget is a node cap, not a time cap, so the saving is wall clock at constant search
   size.

|---|---|---|---|
| `[14,14,14]`/10 | 93.2 s | 71.0 s | 600 |

contradictory. The CPU prediction landed within 1.4 percentage points.

## Cache side effect (favourable)

`workerPool.ts`'s grade cache keys on `(fen, candidateUcis, gradingDepth)` (`:668`). A FEN
carries side-to-move, so from a fixed root a position can only recur at plies of the **same
parity**. Counting hittable same-parity pairs inside each rung group:

| table | groups | hittable pairs |
|---|---|---|
| `[14,14,14]`/10 | {0,1,2}, {3..7} | (0,2), (3,5), (3,7), (5,7), (4,6) = **5** |
| **`[14,14]`/10** | {0,1}, {2..7} | (2,4), (2,6), (4,6), (3,5), (3,7), (5,7) = **6** |

The change loses only (0,2), a return-to-root transposition that is rare and usually a
repetition, and gains two even-parity pairs. Note the corollary: grouping *adjacent* plies
into a shared rung (e.g. a `[14,14,12,12]` shape) buys nothing here, because adjacent plies
have opposite parity and can never transpose. The harness cannot measure any of this — it
has no grade cache at all (`calibration-providers.mjs:43`).

## What this override does NOT license

Phase 195's own words, §9.1, and they still bind:

> A future re-reading of this phase should treat the §2 frontier as a valid application of a
> mis-specified gate, **not as evidence that shallow grading is safe.**

There is **no game-playing strength evidence for this table in either direction.**
Specifically:

- Ply 2 → depth 10 is the largest fidelity change available short of going flat. Stage A's
  flat-10 row shows `same_full_order` 52.4 % and `same_top_move` 90.5 %; per-node error at
  depth 10 is real, even though the *ladder* keeps 14 above it.
- Gate A (below) is a **blunder-resistance** check on 12 positions, not a strength
  measurement. It says this table does not regress on a known failure class. It cannot say
  the engine plays better or scores better.
- The corpus for a much stronger version of that check already exists and is captured as
  [[SEED-129]]: 8,017 rated CC0 lichess puzzles, ~150 of them at the 1800–2600 bands giving
  a ~30 min two-config A/B. That is the recommended instrument if this is revisited.

## Gate A result — the Maia-blindness fixture

Run 2026-07-31 against `fixtures/engine/maia-blindness.tsv` (12 positions, each with a
correct move verified as Stockfish's own depth-20 top choice), 50 nodes / 8 plies / ELO 1500 /
4 procs, scored per `reports/leaf-wdl/accept-rule.md` §4b: a bare move mismatch is NOT a
failure, both the engine's chosen move and the fixture's correct move get ONE independent
depth-18 MultiPV grade converted to mover-POV expected score, and a position regresses iff
`es(chosen) − es(correct) <= −0.05`.

| ladder | exact-move match | regressions | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| `[14,14,14]`/10 | 7/12 | **5**/12 | — |
| **`[14,14]`/10** | **8/12** | **4**/12 | **no regression; one position gained** |

**11 of the 12 positions are identical between the two arms.** The single difference:

| ladder | `cBFTV` chosen | es(chosen) | Δ vs correct | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `[14,14,14]`/10 | `e2g4` | 0.404532 | −0.570916 | REGRESSION |
| `[14,14]`/10 | `e4c6` (= correct) | 0.975447 | 0.000000 | pass |

Both arms were re-run and reproduce exactly on move, match, delta and verdict, so this is a
real ladder effect and not D-07 warm-hash nondeterminism.

**How to read it, and how not to.** The defensible claim is the negative one: **the new table
does not regress on this fixture.** The gain is one position out of twelve, an 8-point swing
on a sample far too small to be meaningful — do not cite it as evidence that shallower ply-2
grading improves tactical vision.

Two limits on the instrument itself, recorded so a future reader does not overrate a pass:

1. **Low sensitivity.** 7 positions pass in both arms and 4 fail in both, so only a couple sit
   near the decision boundary. This fixture had limited power to detect a regression even if
   one existed. That is the argument for [[SEED-129]].
2. **4–5 failures in both arms is expected.** The engine runs at ELO 1500 against positions
   purpose-built from a class Maia is structurally blind to; a practical-play engine at that
   rung *should* miss most forced queen sacs. Note `g687537-p46` — the original
   self-execution case — fails in **both** arms, as it already did with Stockfish leaves.

**Reproducing this run.** `scripts/engine-wdl-leaf-quality.mjs` was deleted by the WDL revert
(`b1764a83`) and is recoverable at `b1764a83^`. It cannot be reused as-is: it A/B'd WDL-leaf
vs Stockfish-leaf arms *inside one process* by toggling `providers.wdl`, whereas a ladder A/B
needs two processes because `GRADING_DEPTH_LADDER` is a module-level `const`. The run above
used a purpose-built adaptation of it with the constant edited between runs, which is Phase
195 Stage B's own documented method ("module constants edited between runs"). Promoting that
runner into `scripts/` was deliberately not done, because [[SEED-129]] would supersede it.

## If Phase 199 regresses

Phase 199's combined sweep is the strength gate and it calibrates against whatever ladder is
live. `reports/grading-ladder/report.md` already names the plan: *"If that sweep shows a
regression, this ladder is what to revert."*

**Revert target: `GRADING_DEPTH_LADDER = [14, 14, 14]`.** One constant, no other change.

Landing this override *before* Phase 199 is deliberate. A ladder change after 199 would
invalidate the fitted strength curve and require a re-sweep, so the window for changing the
ladder cheaply closes when 199 runs.
